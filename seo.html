Match your pages to real customer questions

A customer comparing providers has different questions from someone learning about a service for the first time. Keyword strategy helps organise those questions around relevant topics rather than filling a page with repeated phrases. Your website should explain the services you actually provide, use language your audience understands and give visitors enough information to decide whether to continue reading or contact you.

Connect discovery with a useful destination

Search visibility matters most when a visitor reaches a page that answers the reason they searched. Consider which service pages should introduce your offer, which questions deserve a clear explanation and where an enquiry belongs. Before discussing an SEO project, share your website and the services you want to make easier to discover. Mention your target audience and any business priorities that should guide the conversation. If you serve customers locally, work through our five-step local SEO checklist to separate profile, service-page and measurement priorities.

Get SEO improvements onto your live website

Start by checking which pages describe your main services and whether search engines can reach them. Give each important page a clear heading, a useful description and links from related pages. Keep your business information accurate and your content useful for the people you want to reach. Once changes are published, a sitemap and Google Search Console can help you check discovery and indexing. These steps support a stronger website; search engines still decide which results to show. Choose an SEO starting point for your website

If important pages are missing from Google, start with a technical SEO review. If pages appear but attract the wrong visitors, review customer intent and service-page content. If relevant visitors arrive but rarely enquire, review your offer, proof and contact-page journey.

What an agreed SEO brief should contain

  • A baseline of relevant queries, landing pages and available enquiry data.
  • A prioritized issue list explaining impact, effort and the person responsible for each change.
  • A page map linking each priority service to a distinct customer need.
  • Implementation scope, acceptance checks and a review date agreed before work starts.

Measure useful enquiries alongside search visibility

Compare equivalent reporting periods and separate branded searches from service-related searches. Review relevant impressions, clicks, landing-page visits and qualified enquiries together. An email-button click signals interest; it does not prove an email was sent or a client was won.

BEFORE WE BEGIN

Your questions, answered

Can Reach X guarantee a particular Google ranking?

No particular ranking can be promised. Search performance depends on factors beyond one provider's control. The conversation should focus on relevant priorities, the work proposed and how progress will be assessed.

What information helps you understand my SEO needs?

Send your website address, your main services or products and a description of the customers you want to reach. If you already have search data, mention that too so the discussion can use available evidence.

Should every service have the same keywords?

Each page should answer a distinct customer need. Related services may share vocabulary, but giving each page a clear purpose helps visitors understand the offer and keeps the content useful rather than repetitive.

How much does an SEO project cost?

The scope depends on your website, technical issues, content requirements and implementation access. Share your URL and priorities so ReachX can discuss a written scope and quote. This page does not promise a fixed package price.

How soon should SEO results be assessed?

Set a baseline and agree a review schedule before implementation. Search engines control crawling, indexing and rankings, so a fixed time to traffic or enquiries cannot be promised. Review completed work and relevant search and enquiry trends together.
